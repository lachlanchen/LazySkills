# Learning from accepted 2D scenes

LALACHAN's accepted shellfish-meal and Catch Me shorts are useful benchmarks,
not templates to copy scene for scene. Their production and review notes live
in LALACHAN's `references/videos/2026-10-26-shellfish-lunch-2d.md` and
`references/videos/catch-me-2d-local-h3-20261026.md`. The performance guide is
`references/characters/four-buddy-2d-performance-and-sound.md`.

The meal connects four contributions around gathering, cooking and sharing.
Catch Me uses a small cast, a readable contact and an affectionate role reversal.
Carry forward consistent identities, articulated movement, a concrete need,
a caring response and time to see its effect. Preserve their original films and
documented limitations as comparison evidence. Acceptance does not prove every
planned action or line was rendered, or require the same model and duration.

Small characters need plausible reach. A low table or stable stool may help
when the action calls for it; establish its position and contacts. It is not a
mandatory recurring prop.

When studying a Reel, inspect the whole action and larger reaction/caption
frames before explaining the joke. Separate observations from interpretation
and sharing hypotheses. Public likes alone cannot identify the cause of
engagement. Borrow useful pacing and staging, then build original action,
dialogue and sound for the buddies. ASR does not establish vocal timbre.

For a comforting comedy, ask: what does one buddy notice, what do they do,
and how does the other's expression or action change? Let the moment answer
those questions without an explanatory moral. Keep the prompt focused on the
scene rather than expanding these lessons into a compulsory checklist.
